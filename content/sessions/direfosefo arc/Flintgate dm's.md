> A town living on borrowed time, its spirit as depleted as the mine that once gave it life. Now, a new, darker industry stirs in the depths.

#### **Overview**

- **Population:** ~400 (and shrinking)
    
- **Vibe:** Grim, desperate, and weary. The air tastes of dust and despair. The people are hardened by labor and loss, their hopes worn thin. They are survivors, but their will is breaking under a new, unseen pressure.
    
- **Theme:** Decay and Corruption. The town is dying from the outside in. The physical decay of the abandoned mine is mirrored by a spiritual decay as something sinister festers within it, preying on the town's desperation.
    
- **Secret:** The mine is not just home to monsters. A **coven of Night Hags** has taken up residence in the deepest, oldest shafts. They are not there for gold, but to feed on the despair of the town. They orchestrate the misery—causing accidents, souring milk, and fueling arguments—to make the emotional "crop" more bountiful. They are the reason the monsters are so aggressive; the hags are herding them.

#### **Adventure Hooks & Quests**

- **The Elder's Last Hope:** Moira has nothing left to offer but her gratitude. She begs the party to enter the mine and find the source of the town's curse. She doesn't expect them to return.
    
- **A Father's Grief:** Kaelen offers the party the town's collective savings (a meager purse of silver) to find his son's body and bring back any token for burial.
    
- **The Healer's Mystery:** Mara doesn't understand the new sickness. She asks the party to investigate the mine's upper levels for a source of contamination—perhaps a fungus or tainted water source.
    
- **The Scavenger's Map:** Gideon will sell a crude map of the mine's safer upper tunnels for a gold piece. He warns them not to go deeper than the second cross-cut.
    
- **The Hermit's Theory:** Silas intercepts the party. He believes the evil is sentient and can be reasoned with—or at least understood. He asks them to retrieve a specific type of geode from the deep veins for his research.
